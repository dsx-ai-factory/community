# Launching Open Communities for DSX AI Factory Projects

Open source is more than public code. It also gives people who use the software a way to understand how it is developed, talk with contributors, and share what they need. DSX AI Factory projects are launching open communities and community meetings so developers, users, and potential contributors can take part in those conversations.

These communities are for the people who use and build on these projects: customers, partners, and developers outside NVIDIA. They are a place to share feedback, ask questions, and learn directly from the project maintainers.

## Why we are launching communities

Issues and pull requests are useful for specific changes. Community meetings create space for the conversations around those changes: why a design took a particular direction, what people are trying to do, and which problems deserve attention. They also make it easier for people who are new to a project to listen, ask questions, and participate.

Open source runs on people, not just code. Meeting maintainers and other users face to face builds the trust and relationships that keep a project healthy, and turns occasional contributors into a community.

Community meetings are common practice in open source. Kubernetes special interest groups and many CNCF projects use the same general model. Our communities follow that model with open participation, public discussion, and shared meeting information.

## What we hope to learn

We want to learn from the people who use and contribute to these projects. In particular, community conversations can help us understand:

- How the software is used in real AI factories, while designs can still change.
- Which problems matter most to users.
- Who wants to contribute, and what would help them get started.

## What users and contributors get

Users and contributors can use the communities to:

- Talk directly with people who build the software.
- Comment on planned work and share use cases.
- Bring questions, ideas, and experiences to an open discussion.
- Share what they built or how they run the software.
- Read public meeting notes, recordings, and slides in the project Drive folders.

Meetings are open to everyone under the project's Code of Conduct. You do not need to work at NVIDIA, be a customer, or have contributed before.

## How community meetings work

- **A regular cadence.** Meetings follow a set schedule, announced on the mailing list and calendar, and keep going even when only a few people attend.
- **A public agenda.** Anyone can comment on the meeting notes document to add a topic or question before the meeting.
- **Public notes and recordings.** The public Drive folder holds meeting notes, recordings of online meetings, and slides.
- **Follow-up in the open.** Conversations continue in Slack and on the mailing list between meetings.

## How to join

1. Request an invite to the DSX AI Factory Slack workspace at [inviter.co/dsx-ai-factory](https://inviter.co/dsx-ai-factory), then join the project channel.
2. Join the project mailing list for meeting invites and announcements.
3. Follow the [DSX AI Factory Community calendar](https://calendar.google.com/calendar/embed?src=c_d83d62cba35a7b67c739c701dd77122feefff93389c4cc83b5c2b1d224c07c75%40group.calendar.google.com).
4. Bring your questions, use case, or contribution to a meeting.

## What comes next

NVIDIA GPU Operator, NVIDIA Kubernetes Engine, and NVIDIA Run:ai are the first communities. More communities are on the way, and each will be added to the [communities list](../communities/README.md).
